WHO Z-score engine

Uses official WHO 2006 Child Growth Standards LMS tables. Computes WAZ, HAZ, WHZ, BAZ, and HCZ via the Box-Cox formula. Near-perfect agreement with WHO Anthro software — any differences are rounding only.

MethodWHO LMS (2006)
AccuracyDeterministic
LimitationsMeasurement error → z-score error

Current-status classifier

100% rule-based using WHO/CMAM cut-offs. No ML involved. "Accuracy" is 100% by definition — real-world error comes only from measurement error, not from the classifier itself.

SAMWHZ < -3 or MUAC < 11.5 or oedema
MAM-3 ≤ WHZ < -2 or MUAC 11.5–12.5
StuntingHAZ < -2
UnderweightWAZ < -2

OCR pipeline

Tesseract 4 + OpenCV preprocessing (grayscale, CLAHE, threshold, deskew). Extracts weight, height, age, MUAC from health card photos or PDFs. All extracted values must be human-reviewed before use.

Ethical notes & limitations

  • ChildNutri AI is a screening and education tool. It does not diagnose illness and does not replace a doctor, nurse, or Anganwadi worker.
  • The ML model is trained on survey data; performance may differ in clinical contexts. Treat the risk tier as an early-warning signal, not a clinical verdict.
  • Context features (food groups, meals, wealth) are used only for risk scoring — not stored as personal identifiers.
  • OCR-extracted measurements must be verified by a human before they are used in any clinical decision.
  • WHO z-scores are only as accurate as the measurements. Errors in measuring equipment or technique are not detectable by the software.
  • The system currently does not implement Alembic migrations — suitable for demo and early pilot use only.
